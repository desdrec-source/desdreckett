Should you hire a YouTube agency, a consultant, or nobody yet?

This question usually arrives after a quote. The monthly looks like a salary. That's a signal.

This question usually arrives after a quote.

Someone has been told they need a "content engine." The monthly looks like a salary. The deck is full of subscriber projections. The owner can feel they should say yes because video matters now. They can also feel they're about to buy a second company they don't want to run.

Let me split the options the way I would if we were on a call.

Three different jobs

  • A production person makes the video. Edit, film, thumbnails. Useful when the bottleneck is hours, not thinking.
  • A consultant or advisor helps you choose the questions, the cadence, the metric. Useful when you can publish but you keep publishing the wrong thing.
  • An agency that "runs the channel" tries to do both, and often a third thing as well. Growth. Always growth. Growth toward what is the part that goes missing.

Those are not the same purchase. If you mix them up you'll hire a film crew to fix positioning, or a strategist to fix a Tuesday that has no slot in it.

Red flags I would not ignore

  • Guaranteed results. Nobody controls the algorithm. A promise of X subscribers by a date is a warning. Same with "we'll get you leads" with no definition of a lead.
  • They hold the channel under their account. The firm owns the Google login. They get access you can revoke. If getting the channel back would take a lawyer, don't start.
  • The monthly report is views, posting volume, and a line about brand awareness. For a practice, that's the wrong scoreboard.
  • They want a long lock-in with no clean exit. Video takes time. That's true. A twelve-month trap is not the same as time.
  • They can't answer "what question should this firm own first?" If the first conversation is kit, posting frequency, and a content calendar with themes like "mindset Monday," you're buying a creator machine.

When nobody yet is the right answer

If you haven't published four buyer videos of your own, don't outsource the thinking.

You need to feel the cadence. You need to hear your own answers back. You need to see whether the hours can stay near six. An agency will hide all of that behind a process that looks professional and still points at the wrong room.

Make the first videos. Run the P or B labels. Then decide what's actually stuck. Thinking, hours, or nerve.

  • Nerve doesn't need an agency. It needs a date and a plain first video.
  • Hours might need an editor. That's a small hire. Not a strategy retainer.
  • Thinking might need a second pair of eyes on the questions. That's a consultant-shaped job. Time-bound. Audit. Map. Then you publish.

What I would ask before signing anything

  • Which channels have you worked on for firms like mine, not creators?
  • What changed, in sentences a buyer would recognise, not in view counts?
  • Who owns the login?
  • What does the first month look like in hours on my side? If the answer is "we handle everything," ask who's speaking on camera and who's choosing the titles. If they want to invent the titles without sitting with your actual calls, they'll build a generic channel with your face on it.
  • How do we stop if it's wrong? Get that in the document.

Can you see why that last one matters? People stay in bad retainers because leaving feels like admitting the last six months were a waste. Build the exit while everyone is still polite.

The intern problem, while we're here

A lot of firms don't hire an agency. They hand YouTube to whoever is youngest in the building.

Sometimes that person is good. Often they're being asked to invent a strategy they've never had to live with. They copy what performs in creator-land. You get Shorts, trends, and a channel that looks current. The owner still owns the relationships. The intern can't answer the buyer's actual question on camera.

If that's the setup, the fix is not a bigger agency. The fix is the owner, or the practitioner who does the work, speaking for an hour a week on the questions they already answer. The intern can edit. They shouldn't choose the list.

I have no interest in talking you into a retainer you don't need. I have a lot of interest in you not handing the login to someone who will turn you into a presenter against your will.

Hire production when the thinking is clear and the hours are the problem.

Hire advice when you keep making the wrong videos neatly.

Hire nobody yet if you haven't put four buyer answers on the internet.

And if the quote in front of you feels like buying a TV station, it probably is.
