Shorts or long videos if you run a practice?

Shorts are easy to start and easy to hide in. The long video is the spine.

Shorts are easy to start and easy to hide in.

That's why so many owners begin there. A minute on a phone. A caption. A bit of text on screen. It feels like progress. Sometimes it even travels.

Then they look at the diary. Still quiet. So they make more Shorts. Because that's what travelled.

Here's the split I use.

Shorts are discovery. Long videos are how a buyer decides.

If you only do one, do the long one. The Short can point at it. The Short should not be the spine.

What each job is for

A Short can put your face, or your sentence, in front of someone who wasn't looking for you. That's useful. It's also noisy. A lot of those people were never going to hire a consultant. They were scrolling.

A long video can sit on a search. It can walk through the problem the way you walk through it on a call. It can pre-sell the room. Someone who has just watched you think for eight minutes arrives differently. They've already tested whether your brain fits the problem.

You with me on that?

This is why I get twitchy when a firm says "we're doing video now" and the whole plan is thirty-second clips. That's a feed plan. Feeds reset. Search doesn't reset in the same way.

When Shorts earn their place

Use a Short when you already have a long answer worth pointing at.

Take the one sentence from the long video. The Challenge. Put it on screen. Send people to the longer piece. That's a decent use of the format.

Use a Short when you're in a room and something true comes out of your mouth. Film the sentence. Don't build a second production line around it.

Don't use Shorts as a way to avoid finishing a thought. A lot of "I don't have time for long videos" is "I don't want to decide what the video is about." A Short won't decide it for you. It will just delay the decision in public.

Length, without the mythology

How long should the long one be? As long as the question.

Three minutes if the honest answer is three minutes. Twelve if the buyer needs the working. I'd rather a tight five than a padded fourteen.

Industry pieces often quote a six-to-ten minute window for a proper answer. Treat that as a shape, not a rule. If you're stretching to hit a number, you can feel it. So can they.

And please don't add a two-minute intro about who you are and what you help with. They clicked a title. Start on the title.

The fear underneath this question

A lot of people pick Shorts because long video feels like a performance.

I get that. Sitting down for ten minutes and talking feels exposed. A Short feels disposable. If it's bad, it disappears into the scroll.

The problem is the buyer who's about to spend money doesn't decide on a disposable clip. They decide after they've heard you finish a thought. That's the job the long video does. The nerves are part of the job. They fade. The unfinished thought doesn't become a pipeline just because it was short.

It will feel awkward at first. Stick with the weekly long one. Use Shorts as offcuts if they're genuinely useful. Not as the plan.

What I'd do if I were starting a firm channel this month

One long video a week on a CAT Moment. Search title. Buyer language.

If there's leftover energy, cut one Short from the same session. Same sentence. Point back.

No separate Shorts calendar. No daily promise. No "I'll do Reels, TikTok and Shorts so I'm everywhere." Everywhere is how the hours explode and the estate never gets built.

Six hours a month. Most of those hours should land on the video a buyer would search, not on the clip a stranger might swipe.

What I see when firms only do Shorts

The channel looks busy. The watch history is a pile of half-thoughts. A buyer who clicks the channel name can't find the deep answer. They find more hooks.

Hooks without a landing video train people to swipe. That's fine for entertainment. It's a poor way to sell a six-month engagement.

You also end up chasing the one Short that moved. Then the next ten try to copy the shape instead of the sentence. Shape is not strategy. The sentence is.

If you already have a graveyard of Shorts and no long videos, don't delete the graveyard. Just stop feeding it as the plan. Pick one question those clips kept circling and answer it properly this week.

Audio and the long video

If you're avoiding long videos because of kit, fix audio and leave the rest. People finish a ten-minute answer if they can hear you. They won't finish it if you sound far away.

Phone. Quiet room. Closer than you think. That's most of the production value that matters for this job.

The rest of the polish can arrive later, after the cadence exists. Polish before cadence is another delay with a shopping cart.

Shorts or long? Long, if you have to pick.

Shorts on top, if they serve the long one.

If you want a simple stack for that, it's in the free community. If you want help choosing the first ten long titles, that's a conversation.

You don't need to win the scroll. You need to be findable when the brief is already warm. Those are different games. Play the second one.
