What should I actually talk about on YouTube?

The blank page is not a camera problem. It is a buyer-language problem.

This is the question under most of the other questions.

People ask about cameras and calendars because those feel practical. The thing they're avoiding is the blank page. What goes on the page.

Most coaches and consultants don't have a shortage of things they could say. They have a shortage of things a buyer would search.

Those are different lists.

The peer trap

Coaches and consultants often build a peer channel by accident.

How I structure my week. How I use AI in my practice. Why the industry is saturated. Behind the scenes of the work. A little vulnerability. A little "charge your worth."

Other practitioners watch that. They comment. It feels like momentum. The calendar stays empty.

The algorithm is not broken. You trained it. You clicked peer content. You joined the groups. You copied what performed in that room. Then the system sent you more of the same.

I audit channels for coaches and consultants. The pattern is boringly consistent. The videos that get applause from peers are almost never the videos that create a qualified enquiry.

If your comments are full of "this is so good" from other coaches, and your diary is still quiet, the content is working. It's just working on the wrong person.

CAT Moments

CAT is Challenge, Aspiration, Transformation. In the buyer's words. Not yours.

It's the ten o'clock search. Not the thought-leadership calendar.

Here's how you build one video from it:

  • Write the exact person. Not "business owners." The person who already pays you.
  • Write one Challenge they would admit privately. The one they dress up on the first call.
  • Write the Aspiration in their language. What they want to be true in six months.
  • Write the Transformation as a before and after they can recognise. Not a metaphor. A change they could point at.

Title the video in that language.

Does that make sense? Because once you have those four lines, the video is mostly spoken already. You've said this on calls. You're not inventing a topic. You're recording a sentence that already exists in the practice.

Two columns

Keep this next to the laptop when you plan the week.

Peer topic: How I batch content as a coach. Ten ChatGPT prompts for consultants. Personal brand tips. A day in the life.

Buyer search: Referrals dried up and I refuse to live on social. How to run a discovery call that doesn't feel salesy. Why the team ignored the strategy I paid for. What to do in the first ninety days after the offsite.

One of those columns recruits coaches. The other recruits clients. You get to choose. The platform won't choose for you. It will amplify whichever one you keep feeding.

A worked example

Leadership consultant

  • Challenge: I've been hired. Nobody implements.
  • Aspiration: I want the board to stop treating strategy as a PDF.
  • Transformation: a ninety-day cadence the team actually runs.
  • Title shape: why your strategy deck dies in week two, and the cadence that doesn't.

Business coach for owner-operators

  • Challenge: referrals dried up and I refuse to become an influencer.
  • Aspiration: qualified calls without living on social.
  • Transformation: a YouTube inbound spine that runs in a few hours a month.
  • Title shape: you don't need a personal brand. You need one video a buyer would search.

You can feel the difference when you say them out loud. One sounds like a post. One sounds like a problem.

After ten videos

Open the last ten pieces of content. Label each P or B. Peer or buyer.

If you have fewer than three Bs, that's the whole problem. Not the algorithm. Not the thumbnail. The list.

Then look at the comments, or the DMs, or the "I saw your video" line on a call. Peers saying "so true," or a specific person describing their situation? That's the audit.

Do that before you change kit. Kit won't fix a P-heavy channel. It will just make the wrong videos look nicer.

Search estate

Do this for the handful of questions that already appear on sales calls and you start to own those results. That set of videos is the estate. New videos add plots. They don't restart the map.

You don't need a hundred ideas. You need the seven questions that already pay. Then variations. Then the next layer down when a buyer asks a sharper version.

Buyers are asking LLMs before they ask a directory. That doesn't make a weekly search video less useful. It makes the exact question more valuable. If you already own the result for the thing they type, you're the page they were looking for. Including the page an AI answer might cite.

Don't turn that last sentence into a new religion. Just write the question clearly and answer it like a human who has done the work.

Where the list actually comes from

Don't brainstorm. Mine.

Open the last ten discovery-call notes. Highlight the sentences they used before they got tidy. Those are titles.

Ask the question you ask every client in week one. Whatever they say after the polite version is the Challenge.

Read the emails that arrive after a proposal. The objections are content. "I need to think about it" is not. "The last person we hired left a PDF and nothing changed" is.

If you work with a team, steal from them too. The delivery team hears the real language after the sale. That language is usually sharper than the marketing language.

One warning. Don't turn every internal complaint into a video. Internal complaints recruit peers. Client sentences recruit clients.

If you only do one thing this week, run the P or B labels. Then pick one B and put it on a title.
